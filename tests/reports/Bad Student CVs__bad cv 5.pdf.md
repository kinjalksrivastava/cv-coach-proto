_An Applicant Tracking System (ATS) is software employers use to collect, scan and process applications digitally — many CVs are read by one before a person sees them. ATS compatibility is estimated from four things this tool can actually observe: standard fonts, conventional section headings, ordinary bullet characters, and no table- or image-based layout. It is an indication, not a guarantee — every applicant tracking system parses differently._

### 3. Key areas to improve

- 🔴 **Your degrees have nothing under them but a grade and a date**  
  Add a line or two under each degree where you genuinely have something to show—such as relevant modules, thesis topic and what you did, exchange semesters, certificates, or scholarships—so a reader can see what you focused on and achieved.
- 🔴 **No bullet points anywhere in the CV**  
  Add bullet points under each role and activity to explain what you actually did, how you did it, and what changed as a result—this gives context and evidence of your skills.
- 🔴 **"2019-2025 University of St. Gallen (HSG)" is almost empty**  
  Develop this section with details about your Bachelor’s degree—such as key courses, projects, or extracurricular involvement—or remove the heading if there is nothing to add.
- 🔴 **The CV does not fill a page**  
  Expand your CV with more detail about your studies, activities, and any experience you have—this will give a fuller picture of your background.
- 🟠 **Dates give the year but not the month**  
  Add the month to each date range (e.g., "09/2023 – 06/2024") so a reader can understand the duration of each activity.

_An Applicant Tracking System (ATS) is software employers use to collect, scan and process applications digitally — many CVs are read by one before a person sees them. ATS compatibility is estimated from four things this tool can actually observe: standard fonts, conventional section headings, ordinary bullet characters, and no table- or image-based layout. It is an indication, not a guarantee — every applicant tracking system parses differently._

### 3. Key areas to improve

- 🔴 **Add detail under each degree in Academic Experience**  
  A reader learns which certificate you hold and nothing about what you did. Add a line or two under each degree where you genuinely have something: the modules that matter for your future direction, a thesis and what it was actually about, an exchange semester, a certificate or additional qualification, a scholarship, or a mentoring programme. Only include what is true and relevant.
- 🟠 **Expand experience bullets to show skills and outcomes**  
  Most bullets describe only the task, not the skill or result. For each, clarify what you did, how you did it, and why it mattered, highlighting any transferable skills. Where you have a concrete result or outcome, add it—only if appropriate and accurate.
- 🟡 **Make bullet punctuation consistent**  
  Some bullets end with a full stop and others do not. Choose one style and apply it to every bullet for a more professional appearance.

_Worked examples of weaker and stronger bullet points — Career Services' own, not rewrites of your CV — can be opened underneath this report._

ATS compatibility is estimated from three things this tool can actually observe: conventional section headings, ordinary bullet characters, and no table- or image-based layout. It is an indication, not a guarantee — every applicant tracking system parses differently._

### 3. Key areas to improve

- 🔴 **Add detail under each degree in your education section**  
  Currently, your degrees list only the qualification, date, and sometimes a grade. Add a line or two under each degree where you genuinely have something relevant: key modules, thesis topic and what you did, an exchange semester, a certificate, a scholarship, or participation in a mentoring programme.
- 🔴 **Rework duty-only bullets in work experience to show your contribution**  
  Some bullets in your work experience section only describe tasks or duties. Revise these to highlight what you did, how you did it, and why it mattered, focusing on the transferable skills you demonstrated.
- 🔴 **Add outcomes or skills to experience bullets where possible**  
  Most of your experience bullets describe the task but not the outcome or the skill behind it. Where appropriate, add what changed as a result of your work or which transferable skill you demonstrated.
- 🟠 **Make bullet punctuation consistent**  
  Some bullets end with a full stop and others do not. Choose one style and apply it consistently throughout your CV.
- 🟡 **Grades are shown for some degrees but not others**  
  Showing grades for some degrees but not others can lead a recruiter to assume the missing grade was weaker. Decide whether to add the missing grades or remove the ones you have shown, based on what you are comfortable sharing.

_Worked examples of weaker and stronger bullet points — Career Services' own, not rewrites of your CV — can be opened underneath this report._
